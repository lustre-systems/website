what the book lets slip

The same day, with fewer things missed.

Lustre doesn't ask the practice to work differently. Patients still arrive, get seen, pay and book again. These are the gaps it closes.

Two names on one line, found out when both patients walk in.

A slot can only hold one booking

If a time overlaps another patient's, the booking doesn't save and she's told why. The rule lives in the clinic's database, so no screen can get around it.

"Remind tomorrow's people", written in the margin, done when there's time.

Reminders that come back until they're done

Every evening at 19:00 the app lists tomorrow's patients with the message already written. One tap opens that patient's WhatsApp chat. The notification keeps coming back until the list is cleared.

"Owes 300 (or 500?)" three pages back, somewhere in August.

Every balance, carried across visits

Paying part now and the rest next time is normal, and the app treats it that way. Each patient carries a running balance, and the doctor sees what was charged, collected and still due for the month without reading the book.

Flipping back through last year for someone's number.

Records you can search

Find a patient by name or phone number and see every visit, what was done, what they paid, and the answers to the doctor's own intake questions.

The patient who didn't come, noticed a week later.

Missed appointments wait on a screen

A booking that's still open after its day stays on a list until she decides what happened. The app never marks anyone a no-show on its own.

paper can stay

The doctor can keep writing on paper.

During the visit the doctor writes as usual. The secretary enters it afterwards, in the quiet while a patient is in the chair. Taking payment never waits for that.

If the power goes, both phones still show today's and tomorrow's schedule, clearly marked as not live. The practice can always run a day on paper.

it stays with you

Patient records stay in the clinic.

The records live on a computer at the practice, reachable only from the practice's own phones. An encrypted copy goes each night to the doctor's own Google Drive, in case that computer is lost.

The app never sends a message by itself. Every WhatsApp goes from her own phone, so the clinic's number is never flagged for automated messaging.
